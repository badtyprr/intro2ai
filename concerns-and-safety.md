# Concerns and Safety

Many worries about AI are reasonable. This page lists common ones, says honestly how much each is a real concern, and what you can do about it.

---

### "It will collect my personal information."

**Partly true.** Companies that run AI chatbots store your conversations, and some use them to improve their products unless you turn that off.

**What to do:**
- Follow the postcard rule: never type passwords, account numbers, ID numbers, medical details, or private information about other people.
- In the chatbot's settings, turn off the option that lets your chats be used for training, if you prefer.
- Delete conversations you do not want kept.

---

### "It makes things up."

**True.** AI chatbots sometimes state false information confidently. This is a known limitation, not a rare glitch.

**What to do:**
- Treat AI answers as a first draft or a starting point, not as the final word.
- Check anything important (health, money, legal, safety) with a trusted source or a real professional.
- Week 5 of this plan is entirely about this.

---

### "Scammers use AI to trick people."

**True, and this affects everyone, whether or not they use AI.** Scammers can use AI to write convincing messages, fake voices, and make realistic fake images and videos.

**What to do:**
- Be suspicious of urgent requests for money or information, even if the voice or face seems familiar.
- Agree on a family "safe word" to confirm identity in an emergency phone call.
- Verify by calling the person or company back using a number you already know, not one given in the message.
- Week 2 of this plan covers spotting fakes.

---

### "I will become dependent on it, or forget how to do things myself."

**A fair concern.** Relying on any tool for everything can weaken skills you stop practicing.

**What to do:**
- Use AI for tasks you do not care about getting better at (like formatting a list), and keep doing the things you value yourself.
- You decide how much or how little to use it. Using it for one thing does not mean using it for everything.

---

### "It is too complicated for me."

**Mostly not true.** Using a chatbot is closer to texting than to programming. If you can write a text message, you can use it.

**What to do:**
- Start with Week 1, which has no tools at all.
- Remember that confusion is the course's fault, not yours.

---

### "AI is dangerous or is going to take over."

**This is debated, even among experts.** There are serious ongoing discussions about the long-term effects of AI on jobs, information, and society. Reasonable people disagree on how worried to be. It is not settled either way.

**What to do:**
- You can hold this concern and still learn how the tools work. Understanding something is not the same as endorsing it.
- If this topic matters to you, Elements of AI (in Week 6) covers the societal side in more depth.

---

### "I do not want to use it, and that is my choice."

**That is completely valid.** This plan does not assume you should end up using AI. Even if you never use it, Weeks 1 and 2 will help you recognize AI-made scams and understand news about AI.
