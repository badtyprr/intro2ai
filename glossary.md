# Glossary

Plain-language definitions of terms you will see in courses and the news.

**AI (Artificial Intelligence):** Computer programs that do tasks that usually need human judgment, such as understanding language or recognizing images.

**Chatbot:** A program you talk to by typing (or speaking), which writes back in ordinary language. ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude are chatbots.

**Generative AI:** AI that creates new content, such as text, images, audio, or video, rather than just sorting or labeling existing content.

**Large Language Model (LLM):** The kind of AI behind most chatbots. It learned patterns from huge amounts of text and generates replies by predicting likely next words.

**Prompt:** What you type to the AI. A clear, specific prompt usually gets a better answer.

**Hallucination:** When AI states something false as if it were true. Common enough that you should check anything important.

**Deepfake:** A fake image, video, or voice recording made with AI to look or sound like a real person.

**Training data:** The text, images, or other material an AI learned from.

**Training (on your data):** When a company uses your conversations to improve its AI. Many chatbots let you turn this off in settings.

**Bias:** When AI gives unfair or one-sided answers because of patterns in the material it learned from.

**Machine learning:** The general method by which computers learn patterns from examples instead of following hand-written rules.

**Library card resources:** Free online services, such as LinkedIn Learning, that your public library pays for so cardholders can use them at no cost.
