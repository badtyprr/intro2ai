# Week 5: Checking Whether AI Is Right

**Time:** about 1.5 hours
**Uses AI tools:** Yes

## Goal

Build the habit that makes AI safe to use: checking its answers before you trust them.

## Part 1: Course (about 1 hour)

**If you are using LinkedIn Learning (free with a library card):** search for "responsible AI" or "AI ethics" and pick a beginner course of about an hour. Look for one that covers bias, privacy, and checking AI answers.

- Library AI page: https://www.sjpl.org/blogs/post/learning-digital-skills-artificial-intelligence/

**If you chose Google AI Essentials (paid):** do Module 4, Use AI Responsibly. It includes a checklist for reviewing AI answers. Module 3 (on writing prompts) is optional.

- Link: https://www.coursera.org/learn/google-ai-essentials

## Part 2: Catch it being wrong (about 30 minutes)

AI tools sometimes state false things with full confidence. This is often called "hallucinating." The best protection is to see it happen yourself.

Try these:

1. Ask about a small local detail you know well, such as the hours of a shop you visit or the name of a street near you. Check its answer.
2. Ask for a quote from a famous person, then search for the quote on a regular search engine to see if it is real.
3. Ask a question with a specific number (for example, the population of your hometown), then check it against a reliable source.

## The checking habit

Before you rely on an AI answer, ask yourself:

- **Does this matter?** For a dinner idea, it does not. For health, money, legal, or safety questions, it does.
- **Can I check it?** Look it up in a trusted source, or ask a real expert.
- **Does it sound too confident?** AI rarely says "I am not sure," even when it should.

## Check-in (5 minutes)

- Did you catch it being wrong?
- Does knowing this make you more or less comfortable using it?
